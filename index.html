Product Decisions · context before the flow

Overview

  • One-liner: a way for PMs to catch downstream dependencies before launch decisions harden.
  • Problem: downstream PMs discover breaking changes to their data too late to influence the change cheaply.
  • Solution: Detect likely downstream data dependencies early and give the upstream PM enough evidence to involve the right team before the change hardens.
  • Value: Gives downstream teams time to adapt or push back early, reducing unplanned remediation, launch delays, and product velocity.
Users

Two PMs connected by a data dependency

U
Upstream PM Produces shared internal data

A PM whose team produces data relied on by internal teams, even though those teams are not the product’s primary users.

Outcome they care about: Ship on time. A late-discovered dependency is a threat to their launch date, which is what they’re accountable for.

D
Downstream PM Depends on another team’s data

A PM whose team relies on the data produced by the upstream team.

Outcome they care about: Remediation gets planned instead of becoming an emergency → higher % of delivery on time.

Company type

Where this problem is most acute

  • 1,000–5,000 employees: large enough that PMs cannot be close with every downstream consumer, but not so large that data dependencies are managed by a dedicated governance team.
  • At least 10 independently owned product or data teams.
  • Product teams ship customer-facing changes at least monthly: frequent change means upstream PMs are constantly working through evolving plans, making it harder to identify and notify every affected downstream team early.
Current state

What happens now (status quo)

1

The upstream PM’s team is planning a launch that changes the data relied on by the downstream PM. The launch is 6 weeks out.

2

The upstream PM likely knows downstream dependencies may exist, but early in planning they often lack a reliable, low-cost way to know which teams are actually affected. Identifying those teams and validating the impact requires manual investigation while the proposed change is still evolving, so dependencies often remain undiscovered until implementation makes them concrete.

3

The downstream PM finds out 1 to 2 weeks before launch, when implementation or launch-readiness work exposes the dependency.

4

By then, the upstream technical approach is much harder to change, so the downstream team has little leverage to influence the design.

5

The downstream team may need to rush fixes, accept broken or inaccurate reporting, or force a late change that delays the launch.

Prototype scoping

Hypothesis: If we reduce the effort required for an upstream PM to identify a downstream data dependency and notify the downstream team about it, they will loop in downstream PMs with more lead-time to launch.

In scope

  • Detect a likely downstream dependency.
  • Show the upstream PM enough evidence to trust it.
  • Help the upstream PM notify the downstream PM early.

Deferred / out of scope

  • Post-notification alignment process
  • Technical feasibility assessment.

Product decisions

Should this be a standalone product or live inside an existing tool?

Decision

Start as a standalone product to prove the core detection and evidence experience across tools.

Alternatives considered

  • Jira or Linear feature: Easier distribution, but one platform cannot see all the signals across documents, meetings, tickets, and code.
  • Notion or Google Docs plugin: Detects risk while a PM writes, but over-optimizes for one source when evidence is spread across several.

Rationale

The biggest risk is whether PMs trust the detection and evidence enough to act. Standalone lets us test that core value before optimizing distribution.

Tradeoff and revisit signal

Standalone adds another destination for PMs. If they act on alerts but want them inside existing tools, integrations become the next distribution channel.

How should we define the North Star metric?

Decision

Average lead time of actionable downstream PM awareness — weeks between notification and launch, counted only for alerts where the downstream PM requested alignment or confirmed the impact.

Alternative considered

Average lead time of downstream PM awareness, unfiltered — the same measurement, but counting all notifications regardless of whether the downstream PM engaged.

Rationale

The unfiltered version can be inflated by firing earlier on weaker signals. If the system alerts on half-formed plans six weeks out, lead time looks great on paper, but the downstream PM cannot act because nothing is concrete enough yet. The actionable filter measures only notifications that were worth sending—earliness and usefulness in one number.

Tradeoff

The filtered version is harder to move because it depends on both system quality and downstream PM engagement, not just detection timing. We accept that because lowering detection thresholds should not be able to inflate the metric at the expense of real user value.

Revisit signal

If downstream PMs engage with alerts but still cannot influence the upstream design in time, evolve the metric from awareness to whether their input changed the outcome.

ATAQs

  • Why wouldn’t the upstream team just involve them in the first place?
    • Because the upstream team often doesn’t realize the downstream dependency early enough, and even if they do, they’re human and have other large priorities. Human recognition and memory aren’t a reliable early detection and notification system—an automated system would probably win on detection recall (% of real dependencies caught), which is in the causal chain of our North Star metric.
  • Why it should be a team whose product’s main users aren’t the downstream data teams?
  • Which user (downstream or upstream) should reach out to the other in this workflow?
  • Are there boundaries around which detected dependencies should be shared?
  • Why hasn’t this been solved yet?
